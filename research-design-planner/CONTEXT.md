# Context and Boundaries

## Source

Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2023).
*Research Design: Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed Methods Approaches* (6th ed.).
SAGE Publications.
This skill adapts guidance from chapters 1 through 10 through paraphrase.

## Ubiquitous language

- **Research approach**: quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods.
- **Research design or strategy**: a plan connecting questions to evidence and analysis.
- **Research problem**: the issue, gap, or need that makes a study worth doing.
- **Purpose or aim**: the intended direction of the study.
- **Research question**: the question a proposed study will answer.
- **Integration**: the planned point and method for bringing strands together.
- **Metainference**: an interpretation formed from integrated strands.

## Domain boundaries

The skill guides design reasoning and proposal organization.
It does not supply study data, results, citations, permissions, or ethics approval.
It does not guarantee that a design is feasible in the user's setting.
It asks users to verify program, disciplinary, and institutional requirements that may differ from the book.

## Statechart invariants

1. Intake accepts incomplete goals and uncertainty.
2. Load only the current decision or action prompt.
3. Keep explicit user choices separate from semantic recommendations.
4. Enumerate choice candidates and include an `other` option.
5. Apply a 0.7 minimum confidence to semantic recommendations.
6. Route low confidence, invalid choices, or judgment errors to `CLARIFY`.
7. Do not auto-select a research design when a decision is unresolved.
8. Decision states select or route; action states ask questions or generate prose.
9. Store only relevant, non-identifying study context.
10. Keep participant identities, contact details, and confidential raw records out of decision context.
11. Mark unknowns as open questions instead of filling them with assumptions.
12. Keep the problem, purpose, questions, and method aligned.
